Inversion calculation of inlet concentration in cyclone separators based on the frequency domain characteristics of dynamic pressure in the vortex finder

Li-Xin Yang, Jing-Yi Pan, Han-Gong Zhang, Bo Hu, Jian-Yi Chen

Powder Technology485, Part 1 (2027), 123100

论文于2026年8月11日在线发表，编入2027年卷期；公告按在线发表日期排序。

摘要

Inlet particle concentration is a key parameter governing cyclone separator separation efficiency, yet the lack of reliable long-term online measurement methods has long hindered optimal particle recovery and emission control in industrial practice. This study investigates a PV-type cyclone separator using high-resolution dynamic pressure measurement and high-speed photography to analyze frequency-domain characteristics of pressure fluctuations under gas–solid conditions and their correlation with top ash ring shedding. Results show that the solid phase induces low-frequency periodic pulsations in local static pressure; top ash ring shedding intensifies vortex core oscillation and transmits these periodic disturbances to the vortex finder where erosion is lighter. The dominant frequency extracted from pressure signals inside the vortex finder via Fast Fourier Transform matches the top ash ring shedding period from high-speed photography statistics, with a relative error within 3.3%. Based on dimensional analysis, an empirical model incorporating dimensionless groups such as the ratio of inertia to gravity, macro Reynolds number, and Froude number was established to predict the top ash ring shedding period; the deviation between model predictions and experimental values is within ±15%. By combining the measured dominant pressure frequency at the vortex finder with the shedding period prediction, back-calculation of inlet concentration was realized. This approach shows strong engineering potential under low-concentration conditions (10–50 g/m3) and offers a safe, erosion-resistant, and readily implementable indirect measurement strategy that facilitates real-time optimization of cyclone operation, thereby improving particle recovery efficiency and reducing particulate emissions.
